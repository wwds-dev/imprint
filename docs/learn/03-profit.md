# 3 · Making money with it

> ⚠️ Not financial, legal or tax advice. Income from freelancing, streaming and
> self-publishing is taxable almost everywhere. Set aside 20–30% of revenue,
> keep receipts for API and hosting costs (usually deductible), and talk to an
> accountant once it is more than pocket money.

The full breakdown — per platform, with payout rates and signup links — is
chapter 18 of the README. This page is the decision layer on top of it: which
path to start with, and what actually determines whether it works.

---

## The one thing that decides it

**Nothing here pays for output. It pays for finished, positioned work.**

The agents remove the hours, not the judgement. A generated novel nobody wants
to read earns nothing at any volume; one good book with a real cover and a
correct category earns for years. Every path below fails the same way — volume
without taste — and succeeds the same way: fewer things, finished properly.

---

## Paths by how fast they pay

### Fastest cash — service work
**Client Gigs** (logos) and **Site Builder** (front-end).

You sell a deliverable and get paid this week. Lowest ceiling per hour, but no
audience needed, which is why it is the honest starting point when the balance
is zero. Needs a Fiverr or Upwork seller account, ID-verified for payouts.

The agent's job is throughput: more orders taken, or premium tiers you would not
have had time to offer. It is not a substitute for a portfolio.

### Medium — self-publishing
**Draft → Publish → Market**, then KDP.

Royalties run $0.35–$7 a book. One title is a rounding error; a backlist of
several in one genre compounds, because each new release lifts the others. This
is the path the app is best shaped for, and the slowest to first payment.

Ghostwriting on Reedsy or Upwork pays far better per hour ($500–$10,000 a
project) and uses the same Draft agent — worth doing alongside, since it pays
now while the backlist builds.

### Slowest, most durable — catalogue
**Audiobooks** (ACX, 25–40% royalty) and **Music** (streaming at
$0.003–$0.005 a stream on Spotify).

Streaming maths is unforgiving: 1,000 streams is about $3–$5. Nobody makes a
living on one release. Catalogue does, over years — and sync licensing
($50–$50,000 a placement) is where the real money in music actually sits.

## What to expect, honestly

| Path | Month 1 | Month 6 | Year 2+ scaled |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fiverr logo gigs | $10–$75 | $400–$1,500 | $3k–$10k/mo |
| Freelance web design | $0–$500 | $500–$2,500 | $5k–$15k/mo |
| Self-published books | $5–$50 | $50–$500 | $500–$5k/mo |
| Ghostwriting | $0 (samples) | $1k–$3k | $5k–$15k/mo |
| Music streaming | $5–$30 | $20–$100 | $200–$2k/mo |
| Audiobook royalties | $20–$100 | $100–$500 | $500–$3k/mo |

Month 1 is small on every row. That is the shape of the thing, not a failure —
plan for six months before judging a path.

## Stack two or three

Most of the value is in combining paths that pay on different clocks:

- **Gigs now + books later.** Service work covers the API bill while the
  backlist has no readers.
- **Book + audiobook + companion course.** One manuscript, three products,
  three royalty streams — the marginal cost of the second and third is small.
- **Music catalogue + narration service.** Passive plus active.

Diversify before you optimise. Two paths at 60% beat one at 100%, because one
platform changing its rules cannot end you.

---

## Watch the unit economics

Your costs are real. Before committing to a path, check the ratio:

- A GPT Image logo concept uses a conservative budget reserve in Imprint; the
  gig sells for **$10–$75**. Check the live estimate before generating a batch.
- A full audiobook conversion runs to **dollars** in TTS, against **$20–$100** a
  month early on. Fine, but not free — check the estimate first.
- A long drafting session on a frontier model is the easiest way to spend
  €5 without noticing. Set the daily cap and let it stop you.
- A **long-form video** costs €1–2, mostly images. Against YouTube ad revenue
  that is a slow return; against one client video it is nothing. Know which
  you are doing before you render twenty of them.
- A **30-second social clip** is about **€0.25**. Cheap enough to make several
  and pick one, expensive enough that a daily habit adds up — five a day is
  roughly €35 a month.

All of these count against the caps now, including the ones billed per image
or per character rather than per token. If a rate has never been filled in the
label says so rather than showing €0.00 — an unpriced action is the one that
quietly empties an account.

`Cost History` in the right rail shows where the money actually went. Look at it
weekly — most people are surprised by which agent dominates.

## Distribution is the half nobody budgets for

Every path above assumes someone finds the thing. That is what Social mode is
for, and it is worth being blunt about the arithmetic: a book with no audience
sells the same number of copies whether it took you a week or a year.

The cheap, durable version is one campaign per release, three or four angles,
posted at each platform's cadence for a fortnight. It costs cents in tokens and
a few euros in clips. The expensive version is paid ads, which this app does
not do and which is not where to start.

Three platforms can post from here today — YouTube, Reddit and Pinterest. The
rest are drafting-only, which means the writing is automated and the posting is
you. That is a real limit, not a temporary one: the gates are app reviews and
business accounts, not missing code.

## Accounts you will need

Free to open, all of them: [Fiverr](https://fiverr.com) ·
