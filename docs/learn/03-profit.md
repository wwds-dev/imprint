# 3 · Income science

> **Goal:** choose one narrow offer, state why it might work, measure the full
> funnel, and automate only after observed contribution is positive.

AI lowers production cost. It does not create willingness to pay. The useful
question is not “How much can this make?” but “Under what measured conditions
does one more sale create value without adding equal human labour?”

This chapter is an experimental operating method, not financial, legal, tax,
or platform advice. There is no guaranteed or “passive” income path.

## The five equations

Use one currency and one observation window.

```text
Revenue = completed sales × average collected revenue per sale

Contribution = revenue − platform fees − refunds − AI/API cost
               − direct fulfilment cost − paid acquisition cost

Contribution margin = contribution ÷ revenue

Conversion rate = completed sales ÷ qualified opportunities

Contribution per human hour = contribution ÷ hands-on hours
```

For recurring products, add retention rather than multiplying by an imagined
lifetime:

```text
Observed retention at period n = customers still paying at n
                                 ÷ customers who started the cohort
```

Do not call revenue profit. Fixed expenses, tax, equipment, chargebacks, and
your labour may still sit outside contribution. Record human time even when you
do not pay yourself yet; otherwise “automation” can conceal a low-paid job.

## What counts as evidence

| Strength | Evidence | What it permits |
|---|---|---|
| **Observed transaction** | Collected payment, refund, repeat order, retained subscriber. | Calculate actual unit economics. |
| **Behaviour with a denominator** | Qualified replies, checkout starts, clicks, landing-page conversions. | Diagnose the funnel and run the next test. |
| **Stated intent** | Interview, wait-list signup, buyer request, proposal response. | Build a small paid test, not a catalogue. |
| **External signal** | Search trend, marketplace activity, competitor offer. | Form a hypothesis only. |
| **Model output** | A score, niche idea, price idea, or generated strategy. | Generate test candidates; it is not market evidence. |

Always keep source, date, geography, audience, and denominator beside a metric.
“Five replies” means little without knowing whether they came from eight
qualified proposals or eight hundred generic messages.

## Choose one of four testable loops

These are narrow because each has a different customer, feedback delay, and
automation boundary.

### A. Productised client service

**Use:** Gigs or Web, optionally Creator/Social for the listing.

Sell one defined deliverable to one buyer type: for example, “one responsive
event landing page for independent venues,” not “web design.” Service work is
the quickest loop because a proposal can produce a yes/no signal before the
asset is built.

**Primary measure:** contribution per human hour on completed orders.

**Experiment:** send or publish a fixed number of qualified offers with one
scope and price. Track views → qualified enquiries → accepted orders → approved
deliveries → repeat/referral. Change one element at a time.

**Automate after evidence:** brief template, first draft, concepts, QA checklist,
delivery copy, and order record. Keep scope approval, rights review, revision
acceptance, and final delivery human-controlled.

### B. Owned catalogue

**Use:** Author → Publishing Manager → Audiobook Producer → Social Media Campaign Manager.

Build several related products for the same demonstrated reader need. A book,
audiobook, or music release has a longer feedback cycle and more discovery
risk than a client order. Judge cohorts of releases, not one launch-day spike.

**Primary measure:** contribution per release cohort and sell-through by source.

**Experiment:** validate a hook with a landing page, sample, preorder/wait-list,
or comparable owned audience response before paying to produce a long asset.
Record traffic source, visits, sample actions, completed purchases, refunds,
and follow-on sales.

**Automate after evidence:** profile reuse, outline, drafting passes, packaging,
quote extraction, asset variants, and calendar preparation. Keep editorial
approval, fact checking, rights, store submission, and pricing decisions human.

### C. Content-to-offer funnel

**Use:** Social Media Campaign Manager → Video & Ad Generator → Web Developer, leading to one service or product.

Content is distribution, not the business model. Every post should map to a
single next action you can observe: qualified reply, email signup, sample
request, booked call, or completed purchase.

**Primary measure:** contribution per 100 qualified visits, plus hands-on hours.

**Experiment:** choose one audience problem and three angles. Publish through
the same channel and window, using trackable destination links. Compare the
rate of the intended action, not likes alone.

**Automate after evidence:** angle briefs, platform variants, clip production,
calendar preparation, and reuse of winning claims. Keep community fit, factual
claims, replies, and publishing approval human.

### D. Creator subscription or managed venture

**Use:** Brand Creator → Earnings.

Test one clearly disclosed content proposition for an owned, authorised, or
synthetic profile. Directional trend data can suggest a test; only imported or
recorded account performance can evaluate it.

**Primary measure:** contribution per retained subscriber or buyer cohort.

**Experiment:** define one offer, audience segment, acquisition source, price,
and observation window. Track source → profile visit → paid action → net receipt
→ retention/refund. Record production and messaging time.

**Automate after evidence:** campaign briefs, calendars, safe-for-work teasers,
copy variants, earnings import, and weekly synthesis. Keep consent, age/identity
records, disclosure, direct interaction, content review, and posting human.

## The experiment card

Write this before generating assets:

```text
Audience:          [one reachable buyer group]
Observed problem:  [evidence, source, date]
Offer:             [one deliverable and boundary]
Channel:           [one acquisition route]
Primary metric:    [one rate or contribution measure]
Guardrails:        [budget, time, rights, quality]
Sample/window:     [fixed in advance]
Scale rule:        [what observed result earns a larger test]
Stop rule:         [what result ends or changes the test]
```

The sample and window are operating thresholds, not claims of statistical
significance. For a first service test, 20 genuinely qualified proposals can be
more informative than 500 untargeted messages. For a landing page, 100 relevant
visits may reveal a broken funnel but is still a small sample. Preserve the raw
counts and uncertainty.

## A worked example — hypothetical, not a forecast

A landing-page offer receives 30 qualified enquiries. Three buyers pay €180.
One receives a €30 partial refund. Platform fees are €54, API cost is €12, and
fulfilment takes 7.5 human hours.

```text
Revenue                 = 3 × €180                      = €540
Contribution            = €540 − €30 − €54 − €12      = €444
Contribution margin     = €444 ÷ €540                  = 82.2%
Enquiry conversion      = 3 ÷ 30                       = 10.0%
Contribution/hour       = €444 ÷ 7.5                   = €59.20
```

This does **not** predict the next 30 enquiries. It identifies the current
constraints: acquisition quality, revision time, and whether the contribution
survives a larger sample. If human time doubles at volume, the apparent
automation advantage disappears.

## Scientific discipline for each cycle

1. **Pre-register the decision.** Write the metric, window, scale rule, and stop
   rule before seeing results.
2. **Change one main variable.** Price, offer, audience, creative, or channel —
   not all five together.
3. **Keep the denominator.** Record every qualified opportunity, not only wins.
4. **Separate cohorts.** Do not mix a new price or audience into the old test.
5. **Record failures.** Refunds, rejected deliveries, hidden manual work, and
   unusable generations are costs.
6. **Prefer replication.** A result that repeats across two windows or cohorts
   is more useful than one exceptional day.
7. **Scale one step.** Increase volume or automation gradually and check that
   margin, quality, and policy compliance survive.

## Stop and scale rules

Choose thresholds suited to risk and price before launch. Useful forms are:

- **Stop:** the test reaches its fixed window with no qualified intent.
- **Stop:** completed orders have negative contribution after all variable
  costs, or rework exceeds the time guardrail.
- **Fix:** people reach the offer but do not buy; inspect promise, proof, price,
  trust, and checkout before making more content.
- **Fix:** buyers convert but refund or request extensive revision; repair the
  offer and fulfilment before increasing traffic.
- **Scale:** multiple fulfilled transactions or retained cohorts show positive
  contribution and acceptable quality within the time guardrail.

“Three profitable orders” or “two retained cohorts” can be a sensible first
gate for a small experiment, but neither proves durable demand. It only earns
the next, slightly larger test.

## The automation ladder

Move up one level only when the previous level is observable and reliable.

| Level | What changes | Gate |
|---|---|---|
| **1 · Manual proof** | Human finds demand, makes the offer, and fulfils once. | Someone completes the intended paid action. |
| **2 · Assisted production** | Imprint drafts or renders; human reviews every output. | Contribution stays positive and quality is accepted. |
| **3 · Templates and batches** | Repeated inputs become profiles, briefs, and batch jobs. | Error and revision rates remain bounded. |
| **4 · Prepared distribution** | Calendars, variants, and queues are produced automatically. | Channel metrics and policy health remain stable. |
| **5 · Feedback loop** | Owned actuals inform the next brief and resource allocation. | Data provenance is trustworthy; stop rules still operate. |

Imprint intentionally keeps money movement, external publishing, consent,
client acceptance, and many provider submissions behind human gates. The aim
is not maximum autonomy. It is maximum **reliable contribution per human hour**
without losing control of quality, accounts, rights, or spend.
